Can the law become a pre‑deployment test?

01 THE POLICY GAP

The question is not whether testing matters. It is what testing should mean.

HC 160 / HL Paper 56 concludes that regulators generally lack powers to test or evaluate AI systems before public release, and calls for AISI to receive statutory powers and a pre-release assessment duty.

This project asks how legal duties might become observable model behaviours: concrete enough to test, cautious enough not to overstate what a prompt-based result proves.

02 THE RUBRIC

Six duties, turned into observable behaviours.

Each card is a test family—not a legal conclusion. The prompts are deliberately high-level and exclude publishable bypass techniques.

03 THE METHOD

A small study designed to be auditable.

01

Specify

Publish the rubric, expected behaviour, source mapping and prompt set before running models.

02

Run

A compact Python runner sends each prompt to two named models and saves outputs to a spreadsheet.

03

Judge

An LLM assigns a structured 0–2 score and short rationale against the stated criterion.

04

Check

Hand-score at least 20% of rows and report agreement, disagreement and uncertainty.

$ python runner.py
→ 60 evaluations across 30 test cases
→ results/evaluation_results.xlsx
Human review required before findings are reported.
04 EVIDENCE, NOT A VERDICT

What this prototype can—and cannot—show.

  • Thirty prompts and two models produce indicative evidence only.
  • Results are time- and configuration-specific, not a complete capability assessment.
  • Automated judging is fallible; agreement with human review must be measured.
  • Real statutory testing needs expert red-teamers, privileged access and governance beyond a public API.
